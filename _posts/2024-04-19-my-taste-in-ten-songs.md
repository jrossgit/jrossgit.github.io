I grew up surrounded by music, so here, I've tried to distill the fundamentals of my musical taste into ten pieces of music, starting with...

## *Radiohead* - Pyramid Song

<iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/3M_Gg1xAHE4?si=BZMBvdx2nWhCiFWt" title="YouTube video player" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe>

The difficulty in writing good music is finding the balance between familiarity and experimentation. Of all my favourite bands, Radiohead consistently finds that line, developing a different sound for each album. Pyramid Song hangs its haunting melody over unsettled, yet comforting piano chords. The real musical trick is when the rhythm section arrives, and reveals the shape of the unusual rhythms of the first verse to be more familiar.

## *Claude Debussy* - Arabesque no. 1

<iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/qNoq240t54I?si=YU9gGFBpEVJQHinv" title="YouTube video player" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe>

Pianos are going to play a large part in the choices here - an instrument I grew up with and played as a child. To that end, Debussy is my classical pick. Often, he's less concerned with distinct melodies, preferring to use harmonic palettes and textures to create evocative soundscapes. Here, we're treated to delicate cross-rhythmic melodies, long tension building arpeggiated passages, and warm, stately chords. (The best part is that pulling off the more difficult passages is hella satisfying)

## *Susanne Sundfør* - Memorial

<iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/plikjySlGd8?si=Q_OSzuvg3PN-eGrW" title="YouTube video player" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe>

I found Susanne off the back of *Ten Love Songs*, her deepest dive into pure synthpop. The heavy dancepop tracks are exactly My Kind Of Thing, and some of my all time favourite songs, but then there's Memorial. In this gorgeous ballad, she shows off her powerful and versatile voice, dancing between cutting and cooing tones, before a lush instrumental interplay between strings and piano.

## *Keane* - Somewhere Only We Know

<iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/Oextk-If8HQ?si=T55FMT8TbKcAFhEu" title="YouTube video player" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe>

The nostalgia pick - While all my friends were getting into cool music like Red Hot Chilli Peppers and Eminem, I headed straight for the mid-tempo piano ballads of Keane. Somewhere Only We Know was the first song I'd ever called a favourite: lush, triumphant, and easy enough that I could play it too!

## *Todd Terje* - Alfonso Muskedunder

<iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/WB4SyLujxis?si=UB1BzYkG7hawj_Cr" title="YouTube video player" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe>

If you like bouncy synthpop and have a strong stomach for kitsch, Todd Terje will be a very good time! His, to date, only album is full of playful dance bops that musically explore more than your average dance tracks. Alfonso Muskedunder is a explosive jazz-electronica track that exhibits his best assets - experimental (but not at the expense of being catchy), quirky, but just all around fun.

## *Go_A* - Shum

<iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/lqvzDkgok_g?si=g5yRTxBUQK5Oan5o" title="YouTube video player" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe>

I've watched the Eurovision Song Contest for at least 20 years, but some awful friends of mine enabled the addiction further. It isn't known for bold musical exploration, but the bombastic spectacle more than makes up for that. When a country does go for something unique, it stands out, and Ukraine's 2021 entry is a dynamic fusion of folk and electronica that builds and builds. I love the stupid little triangle hit before the final verse thunders in.

## *Cardiacs* - RES

<iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/gNdnOTvGbJQ?si=FHPJMUjUapwtYNVE" title="YouTube video player" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe>

Cardiacs (not The Cardiacs) are your favourite band's favourite band. You'd be forgiven on first listen for believing a recording studio let a bunch of 7 year olds loose with their instruments, but the more you listen, the more you realise just how tightly composed the chaos really is. They play with everything from unusual harmony, rapid rhythmic shifts, and abrasive instrumentation. After the more conventional verses, RES launches into an instrumental spectacular that won't rest for two seconds, yet it remains seamless and cohesive. They could even pull it off flawlessly live!

## *John Grant* - GMF

<iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/ekFWPsXXcg0?si=L842DDYnN7fHVIPy" title="YouTube video player" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe>

Self belief anthems usually fall flat - a product of a music industry looking to exploit teenage insecurity. John Grant is demonstrably not that: a gay man who dealt with a homophobic upbringing, addictions, and HIV. His version of a self belief anthem is firmly tongue-in-cheek and self deprecating, but very welcoming, and it reads all the more sincere because of that.

## *Fleet Foxes* - The Shrine / An Argument

<iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/9yAxIdkF2Qo?si=GosxxTRNcEjNSkif" title="YouTube video player" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe>

This track encompasses everything about my favourite band. Evocative lyrics, backed by Robin Pecknold at his most expressive; warm, intimate vocal harmonies that flow over a variety of terrains from delicate guitars and tense strings. Songs that make you feel like you've been on a journey are the most rewarding, and by the time the sexophone cacophany dies, I feel like I've been everywhere.

## *Gerald Finzi* - Magnificat

<iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/K_UU-Oy_fA0?si=FvxjLgrGBzl0gt64" title="YouTube video player" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe>

Choral music is something magical - the confluence of pure human voice to create lush soundscapes. Finzi's Magnificat is my favourite choral piece because of the sheer variety of chorus. He weaves the text into the most exquisite melody lines and switches the mood effortlessly - quiet and somber, building a grand, punchy crescendos. Our conductor always told us to sing the Amen "like the most beautiful thing you've ever heard." And he's right. Because it is.
